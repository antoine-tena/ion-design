# The signs of a generic site

A page made by default, by a template or by a language model, gives itself away through choices
nobody made. Each one makes a site look "cheap", whatever its technique. Drawn from Anthropic's
`frontend-design` skill (Apache 2.0).

- A cream background, a serif title and a terracotta accent.
- A near-black background with a single acid green or vermilion accent.
- A newspaper layout: thin rules, square corners, tight columns.
- Everything in identical rounded cards, with the same grey shadow and decorative gradients.
- An overline in spaced capitals above every title, a section title in capitals, "A · B · C"
  strings, "WORD — fragment" labels, an arrow added to links.
- A single word highlighted in a title; numbers 01, 02, 03 when the content is not a sequence.
- A hero of a big figure, a small label and a gradient accent.
- A tinted near-black (`#0B0B0B`, `#111`) standing for black; a monospace font for small labels.
- Every section sliding up as it scrolls into view, a hover effect on every card: one
  orchestrated entrance is worth more.

Each sign is right for some brief: when the brief asks for it in its own words, the brief wins
(a direction of hairline rules and columns, if that is the client's image). Where the brief
leaves a choice open, do not spend it on one of them. To test a plan, imagine the page you
would make for any other client of the same trade: what the two share is the default, change it.
Before calling a page done, remove one accessory.

What makes a page look right is a set of choices: large photos, a marked typography, plenty of
empty space, few colours, measured motion. Boldness goes in one place only.
