# The design brief

What `/design:brief` writes in the site's `design/brief.md` and `/design:direction` reads. The
client's file (`dossier/dossier.toml` when ion-deal created the site) already holds the facts:
name, address, hours, tagline, tone of address, keywords, liked sites, colours to avoid, wished
animation. The brief never copies them: it names the field (`shop.tagline`) and adds what the
design needs and the file does not say. One fact, one place; a changed opening hour never makes
the brief wrong.

The headings below are fixed, in this order; the text under them is in the project's language.
Each statement says where it comes from: a field of the file, a client answer (with its date),
or `hypothesis`, to confirm before the direction.

## Sources

The client's file read (path), the folders looked at (`photos/`, `textes/`, `references/`), the
answers gathered beyond it (who, when).

## Audience

Two or three kinds of visitors, the most frequent first. For each: who they are, where they
read the site (phone in the street, computer at home), what they come for, what they must find
within ten seconds.

## Main action

The one thing a visit should lead to (book online, call, come by, order), then the second.
Where the first one lives today (`shop.links`, `shop.phone`).

## Character

Three words the site must feel like, and for each its excess to stay clear of ("warm, not
folksy"). They come from the client's own words (`shop.tagline`, `texts.story`, the answers),
never from the sector's habits. The tone of address stays in `texts.tone`.

## Fixed and open

What the direction may not change (a supplied logo, an imposed palette or font: the field that
says so) and what it chooses. A creation sold with the site (logo, identity) is open.

## Photos

What the pages can rest on: how many usable photos, their subjects (the place, the hands at
work, the results, the people), their quality, what is missing. A layout led by large photos
needs them; without them, say what replaces them or when the shoot is (`photos.shoot_date`).

## References

- **Liked**: `brand.liked_sites`, not copied; only what the client did not say about them.
- **Sector**: the references the organisation keeps for this sector, when it has a library.
- **Neighbours**: two or three sites of the same trade nearby, and what they all do: the
  sector's default, to stand apart from. `brand.avoid` adds to it.

## Motion level

One number, 1 sober, 2 modern, 3 highly animated (`references/motion-levels.md`), and why:
the wished animation (`brand.animation_level`), never above the level sold (`contract.level`).
When the audience argues for less (slow phones, an audience that dislikes moving pages), say so
as a question for the client, not as a decision.

## To remember

The one thing a visitor should leave with: the angle where the direction puts its boldness.
One sentence.

## Open questions

What the file and the answers leave unsaid, each with who must answer (`contract.approver`).
The direction waits for the ones that change it.
